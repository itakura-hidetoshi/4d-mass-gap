## Design and Chosen Approach

Let a=<Omega_beta,v1>, lambda=norm(T_beta), and r=<((T_beta-T_0)c),v1>, with all objects taken from the unchanged original physical Wilson model.

Certified AOD supplies lambda*abs(lambda*a-r)<=16*C_H*beta^2. Certified AOC supplies abs(r+beta/4)<=8*C_H*beta^2. The actual norm-Lipschitz theorem and ANQ's beta-zero norm give abs(lambda-1)<=C_H*beta and lambda>=1-C_H*beta.

Under C_H*beta<=1/2, lambda>=1/2. Use the identity
lambda*(a+beta/4)=(lambda*a-r)+(r+beta/4)+(lambda-1)*beta/4.
Its three terms give lambda*abs(a+beta/4)<=(161/4)*C_H*beta^2, hence abs(a+beta/4)<=81*C_H*beta^2. This removes the unknown norm weights in an explicitly stated small-coupling regime.

Approaches considered:
1. Reuse the former full-operator remainder. It retains a quadratic budget coefficient and does not accomplish this stage.
2. Keep a rational remainder involving 1-C_H*beta. It is more precise but requires denominator conditions in downstream interval statements.
3. Chosen: use the certified lower bound 1/2 and the rounded coefficient81, yielding short explicit original-model theorems and a linear-budget radius.

At beta<=1/(1024*(1+C_H)), C_H*beta<=1/1024, so81*C_H*beta<=1/8. The actual overlap is <=-beta/8 and strictly negative when beta>0.
